## 5. CRITIC — where the existing plan misreads Melanie or the role

1. **The stack is wrong, and she will notice in 10 seconds.** JD stack is Next.js 15 / React 19 / TypeScript / Supabase / Tailwind / Radix / Stripe / Resend / Twilio / Sentry / Netlify / Anthropic SDK. The plan's NestJS + PostgreSQL + Redis/BullMQ + QuickBooks Online is a different shop. NestJS and BullMQ signal "I will bring my own architecture," which is exactly the "run your world over here" candidate she rejects. QuickBooks appears nowhere publicly (**UNKNOWN**); Twilio appears in the JD, in every consent checkbox, and in her own words ("group text message"). Fix: rebuild on Supabase (Postgres + RLS + server actions), Stripe webhooks, Twilio SMS, Resend email, Anthropic SDK. Show QBO only as an optional adapter, if at all.

2. **She already built the product.** portal.soloselect.com is "The Connected Solo Select Portal": one record for embryos, recips in foal, mares, contracts, invoices, shipments, pickups — copy that reads almost identically to "One horse. Every handoff. Nothing lost between them." A standalone SPADE OPS with its own five surfaces risks reading as "did not look at what we have." The JD explicitly wants a *backend owner* for *her* frontend. Fix: position the prototype as "the backend and the missing pieces behind a portal like yours": RLS policy design for the three personas, RBAC for staff departments, Stripe deposit-to-contract state machine, Twilio status pushes, legacy-record import with validation, and Ask-Spade-style grounded Q&A with a human review path. Mirror her entity names and ID formats (E-26-1842, SS-26-0418, Recip #812, GSA).

3. **"Spade" is customer-facing, not an internal ops copilot.** "Ask Spade" answers contract/shipment/mare questions for customers. The plan's human-in-the-loop framing should be about *grounded answers with a staff escalation path and a beta disclaimer* — her own pattern — not about gating internal workflows behind approvals. Internal Spade: **UNKNOWN**; do not claim it.

4. **She wants receipts, not a landing page.** "Show us what you've built. Live links, repos, shipped work." A "candidate landing page" is presentation; she asked for proof. The deliverable must be a deployed, logged-in, working app (Netlify or Vercel), a public repo with real commit history, tests that run in CI, Sentry wired, and visible Claude Code artifacts (CLAUDE.md, session-derived commits) because the JD says Claude Code must be "part of your actual workflow." A page *about* the build is fine only as the README.

5. **The role emphasizes things the five surfaces may not cover: SMS, payments, auth.** Three of five JD bullets are auth/RBAC/sessions, Supabase RLS, and Twilio SMS in production. If the prototype's surfaces are ops boards without a hardened login, per-persona RLS, an SMS consent + STOP/HELP flow, and Stripe deposit/webhook idempotency, it misses the JD's center of gravity. Auction, marketing automation (Klaviyo), video, and Shopify are *not* in the JD; the auction is a white-label vendor. Do not build those.

6. **She values shipping speed and finish over architecture purity.** "I need a logo today, preferably yesterday"; she ordered the auction site rather than build it; "we're all making it up as we go." A job-queue-and-microservice story will read as slow. What she does judge harshly is finish: clean trucks, mowed grass, "this is good, but it's not the best." Fix: fewer surfaces, every one polished, mobile-first (her staff live on phones and group texts), zero visual rough edges. One broken link or lorem ipsum is a close-the-tab event.

7. **Equine experience and North Texas proximity are "firm requirements."** If Pavan lacks either, the prototype cannot paper over it; the cover letter must address it head-on (relocation/travel commitment, what he has done to learn the breeding calendar, and evidence he read Ep. 12).
