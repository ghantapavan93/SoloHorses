# ADR-023 — The body and the brain: Daysheet is the synthetic estate, the assistant is the product, free and local by default

**Situation.** Research L (2026-09-18) read the existing platform's own public description: breeding schedules, embryo transfers, pregnancy checks, semen inventory, an invoice lifecycle with an immutable ledger, Stripe with ACH and 3-D Secure, QuickBooks reconciliation, a customer portal, contracts, roles, audit logs, rate limits, end-to-end tests. The operation already has the body. What the role and the operation's own assistant point at is the layer above it: understand what is happening across those systems, notice what matters, explain why, remember preferences, prepare the next step, and let the right person approve it. A build that recreates the body more beautifully proves little; a build that shows the brain over a faithful synthetic body proves the thing being hired for.

**Decision.**

1. **Daysheet is the synthetic operational world, not the product.** Its projections, published rules, exceptions, audit trail, simulators and roles exist so the assistant reasons over something real without touching a proprietary system. It is frozen: no surface is added for its own sake, and where it duplicates the body it is simplified, not polished.

2. **The assistant is the product.** In code and on screen it stays `Ask` (the operation's name for its assistant is theirs; the cover email may speak of _your assistant_). Four capabilities, each labelled honestly:

3. **Division of labour, unchanged and now explicit.** Retrieval is for documents only (sale conditions, lease terms, intake instructions, the glossary) and every retrieved claim cites its chunk. Live truth comes from typed tools over projections. Hard rules stay deterministic code. The model reasons, explains, prioritises and drafts. A person owns medical, financial and consequential decisions. The graph orchestrates and never decides.

4. **Free and local by default.** The model is a vendor behind a port (`ModelPort`): the Anthropic API when a key is set, else a local Ollama server when one is reachable with a tool-capable model, else the deterministic offline answerer — the policy gate and the verifier are the same code for all three. Default `qwen2.5:7b-instruct` (Apache-2.0, 4.7 GB, tool calling; two calls per answer: tool rounds, then one call constrained to the answer's JSON schema); `qwen3:4b` where the GPU is small. Measured on the laptop that built this (RTX 3060, 6 GB): the 7B spills a fifth of itself to CPU and took 76 s for the first answer; `qwen3:4b` fits whole and scored 13 of 19 on the eval suite in 12 minutes (25–113 s a case, its reasoning left on because Ollama leaks it into the answer otherwise); `qwen2.5:3b-instruct` scored 10 of 19 in 2 minutes. The suite is the judge, and the honesty page shows the last run. Hosted Hugging Face inference is not on the demo path: its free tier is cents of tokens a month.

5. **The additions, each when its step arrives, all permissive.** Retrieval: pgvector in the existing Postgres (a hand-written migration), `nomic-embed-text` through Ollama, a vendored markdown-header chunker (Mastra, Apache-2.0, with notice), citations verified as substrings of their chunk. Briefs: a BullMQ repeatable job over the review graph; snapshot rows in our Postgres. Evals: `agentevals` and `openevals` beside the deterministic grader, a local model as judge. Traces: OpenInference instrumentation to Phoenix (Elastic License 2.0, a separate service in compose) or Langfuse. The map: `@xyflow/react` with `@dagrejs/dagre`, layout on the server, an ordered list on a phone. Nothing bought, nothing metered.

6. **The visual that says something.** The existing platform draws itself as a ring of capabilities. The brain draws its own boundary instead: the assistant on one side, the records on the other, eleven read-only tools crossing the line, one action reaching a locked gate a person opens, six things it will never do severed at it — and the strands light along the tools a real question reached for (`AgentMembrane`, on the landing from the last real run and on `/future` live from the panel). No mark, no photograph, no name (ADR-019). The causal map for one signal and the trace on a time axis follow the same rule.

**What was rejected.** Rebuilding invoices, ledgers or Stripe flows better than the body already does. A second agent loop (Vercel AI SDK, Mastra) beside LangGraph. A RAG platform (RAGFlow, Dify, AnythingLLM) that bypasses the policy gate. Hosted inference with a free tier that ends mid-demo. Naming the product after the operation's assistant.

**Cost.** One more provider adapter and its spec; a lean prompt to keep in step with the full one; slower answers on a laptop than from an API, said on the screen. Each capability above adds a table or two and a job; none adds a horse-management feature.

**Would change it.** Learning what the operation's assistant already does. If it already briefs, the morning brief becomes an input to it, not a rival; if it already investigates across systems, the causal map and the evidence discipline are what this build adds. The division of labour does not move.
