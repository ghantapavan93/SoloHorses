# ADR-018 — The front door is one mare's story

**Situation.** The first public page led with "Break the system" and a lab of six controls. Two outside reviews said the same thing: a founder does not read a lab, and a lab does not show whether the builder understood the work. The posting's own words are "show us what you've built", from someone who runs a recipient herd of over two thousand mares.

**Decision.** `/story` is the front door and needs no sign-in: it renders as a fixed, seeded reviewer identity (`usr_demo_reviewer`, admin, synthetic data only). It tells one recipient mare's season as a single timeline — set-up, clearances, the transfer, the 14-day check, the day-24 heartbeat that issues the lease fee, the ACH that settled it, Stripe's delivery, the books — with a source badge and a correlation id on every row, and the three exceptions that touch her interleaved where they happened. Clicking a row opens the evidence: the rule that fired, the source records, the trace. The two failure controls that matter to the story sit on the rows they affect: deliver the webhook again (the counters move, the payment count does not) and make QuickBooks unavailable (the sync parks, nothing is lost, it restores itself). The assistant is grounded on the same records, beside the timeline, with four suggested questions that prove abstention, refusal, injection-safety and proposal. `/build` is the honesty page. The lab, the board, the architecture page and the evals stay for engineers, under an "Engineering" heading.

**Cost.** A public route that reads as an admin. It is read-mostly: the only writes it can cause are the two labeled lab controls, feedback, requests and proposal decisions, all audited under the reviewer identity, all on synthetic data, and the seed fixes the identity so a reseed cannot orphan it. The seed and the detectors must agree on titles and dedupe keys or the page changes under a reader within a minute; the seed now computes exception titles with the same rule functions the detectors use.

**Would change it.** A real deployment would put the story behind a share link with an expiry, or remove the reviewer identity entirely; the page itself would not change.
