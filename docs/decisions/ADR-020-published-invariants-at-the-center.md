# ADR-020 — The published invariants at the center; the existing estate owns the writes

**Situation.** Research pass J (2026-09-17) read every public page again and found the rules the operation itself publishes: settlement by the Monday after the sale, 5 PM CST; no registration certificate released on sale day, and none until payment clears; a recipient sold with a foal in utero comes back after weaning, open and in good health, or the buyer owes a $6,000 recipient purchase fee; a leased mare is video-confirmed in foal within three days of leaving; a $1,000 deposit at reservation and a $1,000 implant fee per attempt, the first covered by the deposit. The build had modelled the barn's clinical rhythm (checks, clearances, milestone fees) but not these. They are the rules a sale office and a recip farm actually live by, and every one of them is a boundary between two systems — the ledger and the bank, the vet's record and a fee, the sale platform and a piece of paper.

**Decision.** These invariants become the center of the demonstration, as pure functions in `packages/domain` (`evaluateRegistrationRelease`, `settlementSourcesAgree`, `evaluateDeparture`, `evaluateRecipReturn`, `implantFeeFor`), detectors that ask them about every row (`PAPERS_HELD`, `SETTLEMENT_CONFLICT`, `DEPARTURE_UNCONFIRMED`, `RETURN_ASSESSMENT_MISSING`), a consumer that acts on one of them (`PaymentSucceeded` → the rule runs → a document becomes `ELIGIBLE`, with the rule's name in the audit row), and one person's click that finishes it (`POST /documents/:id/release`, billing or an admin, the rule asked once more, audited by name). Nothing releases papers automatically. Nothing charges the $6,000. Nothing decides a fee from a missing record: the rule says when a person _can_ decide, and on what evidence.

Every exception now carries two more things than its title: the system state as the tables hold it (`payment.status processing · document.status held · rule RegistrationReleasePolicy v1`) and what that means in the words of the person who acts ("the bank has not finished moving the money yet; the papers stay held, and even then a person sends them"). Both are templates over the detector's detail, never generated.

The second front door (`/settlement`) is one sold lot from the hammer to the papers, no sign-in, with two simulated controls that say so on the button (the bank finishing the debit; a late, contradicting delivery) and one real one (release). The lab's list puts the four controls the sale's own workflow needs first — a replayed webhook, an ACH that settles, a provider that throttles, two sources that disagree — and keeps the platform's own failure modes after them.

**The framing.** The operation runs several applications today, and this build does not claim it lacks any of them. The existing applications own the writes. This layer reads projections of them (labelled _Synthetic Equine / Recipient / Vet projection_ on the honesty page), runs the published rules, raises what needs a person, explains it in two registers, proposes, and a person acts — in the system that owns the record. The assistant follows the same line: it may explain why papers are held and may say the rule finds them eligible; it may never say they are released, and when the ledger and the payment provider disagree it abstains (`SOURCES_DISAGREE`: "billing review is required") rather than pick a source. Sensitive financial data is refused in code (`FIN-DATA-03`): full card numbers, account and routing numbers and government ids are not stored and no tool returns them; the refusal names what _is_ available.

**What is fact and what is not.** Settlement timing, papers-until-cleared, the in-utero return condition and its $6,000 fee on the sale, the leasing deposit and implant fee, the three-day video are cited public facts (`docs/research/A-solo-domain.md`, `J-site-audit-2026-09-17.md`). The lease's own late-return amount is "the mare purchase price" and the $6,000 there is an assumption (A8). That a sale settlement moves as an ACH debit through the payment provider is an assumption (A10): the public conditions list bank check, card, cash and certified check, and the rule reads the same for any of them — initiated is not cleared. When an in-utero lot's mare foals and is weaned, and that an implant attempt is counted per embryo, are assumptions (A11, A12).

**Cost.** Two tables (`SaleLot`, `Document`), three horse dates, four exception kinds, two abstention reasons, one more tool, one more consumer, and a second public page. The seed and the detectors must agree on four more titles; the seed computes them with the same functions. The story mare's timeline gains a deposit, an implant row and a planned move North (F19, day ≈ 300 as an assumption), which is where the two front doors meet.

**Addendum (2026-09-18, the hardening pass).** The money's states are a table, not a rank. `PAYMENT_TRANSITIONS` in `packages/domain` lists which payment status may follow which; `paymentTransition()` answers with a domain code, and a provider event that does not apply is ignored rather than applied. `RETURNED` is terminal: a debit the bank returns never clears again, the invoice reopens, an eligible document is held again, and a document already released becomes `PAPERS_RELEASED_FUNDS_RETURNED` — critical, owned by a person, because software cannot recall paper (A17). The API refuses an impossible transition with 409 `INVALID_PAYMENT_TRANSITION` and the evidence ids, so a double click and a replayed request meet the same wall server-side.

**Would change it.** A conversation with the sale office. If papers are released on something other than cleared funds, if the office bills the implant fee on a different event, if the return assessment is a form rather than a clearance, the rule changes in one file and its test, and the pages follow. The framing does not change: rules in code, records as evidence, a person at the end.
