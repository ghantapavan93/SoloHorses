# ADR-019 — The visual reference lock: real products, one job each

**Situation.** A generated interface looks generated: cards because cards are easy, colour as decoration, a chat window where a tool should be. The earlier decision (research G, ADR-001's spirit) fixed the tokens — warm neutrals, one maroon accent that is never a status, four status colours — but not the shape of the screens.

**Decision.** Four finished products are the references, and each has exactly one job. **Linear** is the application shell: one near-black surface, a dim sidebar that reads as part of the page, 32 px rows with a quiet hover, borders at eight percent, drawers for detail, a command menu, keyboard first. **Raycast** is the assistant: ⌘K is one surface — type a code and jump to the record, type a question and Ask answers inside the same palette, compact, then goes away with Escape; ⌘J opens it already in Ask. The assistant never owns a screen of its own except beside the story. **Midday** is money: one row per invoice with one mark per system (ledger, Stripe, books), and a per-invoice timeline of every step with its source and its timestamp; no chart unless it means something, no KPI cards — a line of numbers. **Palantir Foundry** is the _thinking_ behind the lab, not its look: a system-health readout an operator scans in three seconds (name · state), the number of jobs in flight, the number lost, and failure injection as a numbered list, not a wall of buttons. **The operation's own identity** decides colour, type and tone — and only as a palette and a register, never a mark, a photograph or a name (ADR-007's sibling rule in `CLAUDE.md`).

Dark is the default and light is a cookie away: the shell and the front door read as one surface, and the office at six in the morning keeps its light theme. The landing is editorial — a headline, a field of contour lines that is not a map, three unlabelled-by-place nodes for the three sites, a few pixels of depth on mouse move — and nothing else moves. Product name and assistant name stay `Daysheet` and `Ask`.

**The page rhythm.** A static prototype supplied on 2026-09-17 (one HTML file, hard-coded records, scripted answers) set the front door's rhythm and its warm palette: an eyebrow with a copper rule, a two-line headline with an italic serif accent, the three sites as tilted cards on an abstract terrain, then numbered sections — what needs a person, money should be boring, don't trust the demo, small surface serious boundaries — each an application window. The rhythm, the palette and the copy that was generic were taken. Its product name, its assistant name, its glyph, its site names, its fake records and its scripted assistant were not: every window on the page is rendered from the API as the demo reviewer, and every window opens the real screen.

**The test applied to every screen.** Does it look like a generated dashboard? Does it hold a card because cards are easy? Could it belong to any startup if the logo changed? Does the animation say something? Does a founder understand the important state in three seconds? A "no" on the last, or a "yes" on the first three, means redesign.

**Cost.** Four references to keep straight; a reviewer who knows them will see the lineage. The unified ⌘K adds one state machine (search → ask) that the sheet did not have. Dark by default puts the burden of contrast on the tokens, which is why the borders and the muted text were re-tuned rather than inherited.

**Would change it.** A real brand system from the operation would replace the palette and type without touching the structure; that is the point of keeping identity and structure in separate layers.
