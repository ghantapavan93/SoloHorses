# ADR-017 — AI proposes, people approve, rules execute

**Situation.** ADR-007 made the assistant read-only, with "Send to team" as its only way to cause anything. That is safe and it is also where most assistants stop being useful: "hold R-0048 for E-26-0054 instead" is exactly the kind of request a person at the recip farm would want handled, and typing it into a form again is the friction the operation already lives with. An approval gate that lets the model act was rejected in the first round because a gate is a thing to be talked past.

**Decision.** A `Proposal` is a row, not a permission. The assistant's `proposeAction` tool writes one (kind, payload, rationale, evidence, who asked) and previews the domain rule so the card shows the verdict as things stand. Nothing else changes. A person with the right role approves or declines on the same card; approval calls the same service the UI calls (`assignPlannedRecipient`, marked `via: 'AI'` in the audit row), which runs the rule again at that moment. If the rule refuses — the recip was taken in between, the clearance lapsed — the proposal is recorded as _approved by a person, refused by the rule_, and the record is untouched. Declines carry a note. The policy layer still refuses money and clinical requests before any tool runs; proposals exist only for actions that have a deterministic rule to execute them. Answers that created a proposal are never served from the answer cache.

**Cost.** One more thing on the board to look at; a proposal nobody decides on is noise, so proposals are listed with their age and the detectors do not depend on them. Each new proposal kind needs its own rule and its own executing service; there is no generic "apply this JSON to the database".

**Would change it.** If the operation wanted the assistant to act without a person for some narrow, reversible case (a note, a tag), that case would still go through a rule and an audit row; the approval step would be the only thing removed, per kind, in code.

**Addendum (2026-09-18, the release pass).** A proposal is bound to the records it was made from. `Proposal.stateHash` fingerprints what the executing rule reads at the moment of the proposal (for a hold: the embryo's status and current hold, the recip's status, what else is written down for her, what she carries). Approval recomputes it first; if the records moved in between, the proposal is recorded as _approved by a person, refused as stale_ (`proposal.stale` in the audit trail, `PROPOSAL_STALE` to the caller), nothing runs, and the person asks again against the records as they now stand. A proposal whose records did not move still meets the rule again at approval; the fingerprint is the earlier, cheaper wall, and it makes "approve" mean "approve what I was shown".
